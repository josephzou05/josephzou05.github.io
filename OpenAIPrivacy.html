OpenAI and the Ethics of Data Scraping

By Joseph Zou · March 28, 2025

The rise of artificial intelligence has ushered in a wave of transformative innovation, but it has also surfaced complex ethical questions. A particularly important case emerged in 2023 when OpenAI faced a lawsuit for allegedly using massive amounts of personal and copyrighted data without consent to train its AI models.

Background

OpenAI, originally a nonprofit, shifted to a for-profit model and expanded its data-gathering practices. Plaintiffs in a California lawsuit argue that OpenAI scraped the internet for sensitive data — including chat logs, emails, medical records, and creative works — without notifying or asking permission (Cerullo & Ivanova, 2023).

One report highlighted that OpenAI sought out “essentially every piece of data exchanged on the internet it could take—without notice to the owners or users” (Cerullo & Ivanova, 2023).

The Ethical Tradeoff

OpenAI’s tools provide real social benefits in education, healthcare, and communication. Supporters argue that using public data for innovation can be ethically justified because the outcomes benefit society as a whole. But those benefits must be weighed against the autonomy and fairness owed to the individuals whose data was taken.

“OpenAI bears ethical responsibility for training its AI models on personal and copyrighted data without consent.”

Autonomy

Autonomy requires that individuals have informed control over their data. OpenAI users were never given clear notice or the option to consent. By scraping content from blogs, forums, and social media, OpenAI denied people the ability to decide how their digital identities and expressions were used.

Fairness

Fairness requires that those who contribute value to a system are recognized or compensated. Authors, artists, and everyday internet users generated the content that trained ChatGPT and DALL-E. Yet OpenAI profited while contributors remained uncompensated and uninformed (Yang, 2023).

Conclusion

Innovation cannot come at the cost of autonomy and fairness. OpenAI’s methods violated user rights by stripping individuals of consent and failing to provide recognition or compensation. Moving forward, AI companies must commit not only to advancing technology, but also to respecting the people who make that progress possible.
