Building an NBA Prop Predictor

By Joseph Zou · December 2025

I have been a Golden State Warriors fan for as long as I can remember. Some of my earliest memories of basketball are tied to watching games with my family, sitting together and reacting to every run, every missed call, and every big shot. As I got older, that turned into going to games with friends, building routines around tipoff times, and following the team season after season. I still have a mini Klay Thompson shirt from the 2016 season, a reminder of a stretch when being a Warriors fan felt unforgettable.

That connection never faded. Even as life got busier, I kept watching games closely. I paid attention to rotations, matchups, and how different players showed up on different nights. What always stood out to me was how unpredictable the game could be. A player could look unstoppable one night and struggle the next. Someone might explode for forty points against a strong defense and fall short against a weaker one. The more I watched, the more curious I became about what actually drove those outcomes.

Why Player Props

That curiosity deepened when I was introduced to NBA player props. Instead of focusing on wins and losses, props forced me to think more narrowly and more carefully. How many points would a player score? How many minutes would they play? Would the matchup matter more than recent form? Suddenly, each game felt less like a single story and more like a collection of small, measurable questions.

At first, I approached props the same way most fans do, relying on intuition. If a player had been hot for a few games, I expected that trend to continue. If a defense was ranked poorly, I assumed scoring would be easier. Sometimes this worked. Other times, it failed completely. A player would get into foul trouble early. A blowout would limit minutes. A coach would change rotations without warning. The more I paid attention, the more fragile intuition started to feel.

Curiosity Turning Into Questions

What interested me was not whether a pick hit or missed, but why. I wanted to understand which variables actually mattered and which ones only felt important because they were easy to notice. Was recent scoring more predictive than season averages? How much did opponent strength really influence outcomes? Could minutes be anticipated in a reliable way, or was that where most of the uncertainty lived?

Those questions are what eventually led me to build my NBA Prop Predictor. I wanted to move beyond surface level analysis and test my assumptions more systematically. I began treating each game as a data point rather than a narrative, collecting player game logs, matchup data, and contextual features to see what patterns held up over time.

From Fan to Builder

As I worked on the project, my relationship with the game changed. Watching basketball became less about expectation and more about observation. I paid closer attention to usage rates, efficiency, and rotations rather than just point totals. I started to see how many things have to align for a player to hit a certain number on a given night.

Building this project allowed me to connect something deeply personal to something analytical. Basketball was never just a sport I watched. It was part of my upbringing, my friendships, and my identity as a Warriors fan. NBA props gave me a way to translate that lifelong interest into a concrete problem, one that blended curiosity, intuition, and data.

This essay captures why I cared enough to start. The project itself explores what I learned once I tried to answer those questions more seriously.
