Gym Culture and Body Dysmorphia: Survey Results

By Joseph Zou · September 2025

This page summarizes the results of my survey-based study on gym culture, social media, and body image. The analysis focused on eight targeted comparisons that capture how social media exposure, physique comparison, and gym habits connect with body satisfaction and dysmorphic tendencies.

Study 1: followsInfluencersNum vs needsMoreMuscle

Respondents who followed more fitness influencers were significantly more likely to report feeling they needed more muscle. This suggests that influencer-driven content amplifies perceptions of inadequacy and pushes gym-goers toward unrealistic goals.

Study 2: followsInfluencersNum vs comparesPhysiqueNum

Following influencers was strongly linked with comparing one’s own physique to others. This highlights how curated social media feeds fuel constant comparison cycles.

Study 3: comparesPhysiqueNum vs bodySatisfaction5

Frequent comparison to others correlated with significantly lower body satisfaction (negative correlation, R ≈ -0.40). This confirms that comparison undermines self-image and confidence.

Study 4: comparesPhysiqueNum vs feelsTooSmall

Those who compared their physique more often also reported stronger feelings of being too small. This reinforces how comparison creates distorted self-perceptions even among individuals with healthy builds.

Study 5: comparesPhysiqueNum vs feelsCulturePressure

High levels of physique comparison were associated with increased feelings of cultural pressure to look a certain way. This demonstrates how social standards of fitness amplify dysmorphic thoughts.

Study 6: comparesPhysiqueNum vs avoidsPostingDueToPhysique

Respondents who frequently compared their bodies to others were far more likely to avoid posting pictures online because of dissatisfaction. This shows how body image struggles spill into social media behavior.

Study 7: comparesPhysiqueNum vs mirrorChecksPerDay

More physique comparison was linked to higher mirror-checking frequency. This obsessive behavior is one of the strongest behavioral signals of body dysmorphia.

Study 8: bodySatisfaction5 vs avoidsPostingDueToPhysique

Lower body satisfaction was directly tied to avoiding posting on social media. People who scored themselves lower on satisfaction were far more likely to hide online, further illustrating how internal struggles with self-image shape external expression.

Conclusion

Across all eight comparisons, a consistent theme emerged: comparison and social media exposure are powerful predictors of dissatisfaction and dysmorphic tendencies. While the gym can provide structure and progress, the surrounding culture—especially online—often reinforces feelings of inadequacy. The strongest effects appeared in how comparison behaviors translated into dissatisfaction, avoidance, and compulsive habits like mirror-checking.
