Body Dysmorphia and Gym Culture: Survey Results

By Joseph Zou · September 2025

Introduction

This study explores the relationship between gym culture, social media, and body image. I collected over 100 survey responses to better understand how habits and online exposure influence body satisfaction and perceptions of self-image.

Hypotheses

  • Higher social media use would correlate with lower body satisfaction.
  • Greater gym frequency would correlate with higher body satisfaction.
  • Social media would be a stronger predictor of dysmorphia than demographics alone.

Data Collection

The data was collected using a Google Form survey, targeting individuals across gym communities and social networks. Respondents provided demographic information, gym habits, and self-reported body satisfaction on a 1–10 scale. The final dataset contained over 100 entries, which were cleaned and analyzed using Python (Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib).

Regression Analysis

Regression models identified social media exposure and gym frequency as key predictors of body satisfaction.

Key Findings

  • Gym frequency had a positive but moderate correlation with body satisfaction (R ≈ 0.3).
  • Social media use had a stronger negative correlation with satisfaction (R ≈ -0.45).
  • Regression results showed social media exposure explained more variance than demographics.

Reflection

The results confirmed what I had personally experienced: spending time on social media, constantly comparing physiques, is more damaging than helpful. While going to the gym provides a sense of control and incremental progress, social media feeds often distort expectations. This project allowed me to quantify those feelings and show how widespread they are in gym culture.
